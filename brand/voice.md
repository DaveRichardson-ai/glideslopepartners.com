# Voice

We write the way a senior practitioner talks to a CFO they respect: directly, with specifics, and without selling.

## Principles

- **Specific over general.** Name the clause, the regulator, the transaction, the number. "TSAs that support critical operations are material arrangements under CPS 230" beats "regulatory complexity is increasing".
- **Substantiated or omitted.** Every figure, claim and client reference must be something we can stand behind. If it cannot be sourced, it does not go in.
- **Plain words.** Write "use", not "leverage"; "help", not "facilitate"; "complete", not "robust".
- **Commit to a view.** Say what we think and why. Do not balance every sentence with its opposite.
- **Short sentences, varied rhythm.** Most sentences under 25 words. Break a long run with a short one.

## Conventions

- Australian English: organisation, programme (but computer program), analyse, licence (noun), favour.
- Dates as 9 November 2026; times as 2.30 pm AEDT; currency as A$7.6 billion or $13 million where AUD is obvious.
- Regulators and standards by their short names after first use: APRA, ASIC, AUSTRAC, CPS 230.
- Headings in sentence case. No title case, no exclamation marks.
- "Glideslope Partners" in full on first mention, "Glideslope" after. Never "GP" or "Glideslope Partners Pty Ltd" outside legal documents.
- Partner titles: Managing Partner, Founding Partner, and Technology Strategy.

## Words we avoid

Leverage, harness, robust, seamless, comprehensive, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, synergise, unlock, journey, ecosystem, landscape, delve, navigate (except for ships), pivotal, crucial, holistic, end-to-end (unless literally describing a process from start to finish).

## Track record

When we describe past transactions, we say what our partners did in prior roles, and we say so: "transitions our partners have led or held leading roles in". We name a client only where the transaction is public.
