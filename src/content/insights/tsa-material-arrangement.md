## The grace period is over

CPS 230 commenced on 1 July 2025. Contracts in place before that date had relief until the earlier of their next renewal or 1 July 2026, and that relief has now ended for all of them. A TSA signed on or after 1 July 2025 had no relief and had to comply from signing. Extending a pre-commencement TSA counted as a renewal and ended its relief early.

Transitional service agreements were rarely drafted with CPS 230 in mind. Many in force today were signed before the standard commenced, or were drafted quickly at signing as schedules to the sale agreement. If a TSA supports a critical operation, it is a material arrangement and must meet the same requirements as any other material arrangement.

TSAs were already within APRA's outsourcing regime before CPS 230. CPS 231 treated an arrangement with another party, including a related body corporate, to perform a business activity on a continuing basis as outsourcing, so a TSA covering a material business activity needed a compliant agreement and an APRA notification. CPS 230 changes the test, from the materiality of the activity to reliance for a critical operation, and adds minimum contract terms, an annual register submitted to APRA and inclusion in business continuity testing.

## Why a TSA counts

CPS 230 does not carve out arrangements between related or formerly related parties. A material arrangement is one the entity relies on to undertake a critical operation, or one that exposes it to material operational risk. CPS 230 also prescribes some services as material unless the entity can justify otherwise: core technology services for all entities, and underwriting, claims management, insurance brokerage and reinsurance for insurers. A TSA under which a former parent runs the buyer's core banking, policy administration, member registry, payments or data centre meets that test on day one.

The consequences follow directly:

- The TSA provider is a material service provider and belongs on the entity's register.
- The agreement needs the contractual content CPS 230 requires of any material arrangement, including defined services and service levels, APRA access and on-site visit rights, notification of material fourth parties, provider liability for subcontractor failure and a force majeure provision.
- APRA must be notified within 20 business days of entering into or materially changing an agreement for a service the entity relies on to undertake a critical operation. Signing a TSA, extending it, or changing its service scope can each trigger that notice.
- Offshoring through a TSA (for example, where the former parent runs the service from an offshore hub) requires notice to APRA before the agreement is entered into, or before a significant change to it.
- The TSA must sit inside the entity's business continuity planning and testing. The entity sets tolerance levels for the critical operations the TSA supports, and the arrangement must support the entity's ability to execute its business continuity plan.

Both sides are affected. The buyer relies on the services. The seller, if it is itself APRA-regulated, may depend on reverse TSAs for services the divested business keeps providing back.

## CPS 230 requirements compared with common TSA terms

TSAs are written to keep a business running for twelve to twenty-four months while the buyer builds its own capability. They are usually short, priced at cost-plus and light on vendor management terms. The table compares CPS 230 requirements with positions commonly found in TSAs.

| CPS 230 requirement | Common TSA position |
| --- | --- |
| Defined services and service levels | Services described as "as provided in the 12 months before completion", with no measurable service levels |
| Performance reporting | Monthly governance meeting, no reporting obligation |
| Data ownership, location and return | Data handled under the sale agreement, not the TSA; return mechanics unclear |
| Audit and access rights | Limited or absent; APRA access rights rarely spelled out |
| Subcontracting notice and liability | Silent on notification of the provider's own vendors, and liability for their failure excluded or capped |
| Business continuity and force majeure | "Same standard as for the provider's own business", with no link to the entity's tolerance levels and no force majeure provision identifying which obligations continue |
| Termination and transition-out | Exit dates fixed, but transition-out assistance thin and priced separately |

Data return is the item most often left to the end of the term. When a TSA ends, the buyer needs its data back in a usable form, and the seller needs to delete what it no longer has a right to hold. If the TSA does not say how that happens, it is negotiated under time pressure at exit.

## What needs to be in place

### Recipient of TSA services

- Map each TSA service to the critical operations it supports and record the provider on the material service provider register
- Notify APRA within 20 business days each time the TSA is signed, extended or re-scoped, and keep a record of each notification
- Agree measurable service levels and a reporting pack for the services that support critical operations
- Document data location, access and the return-and-destroy process at exit
- Include the TSA services in business continuity testing, against the tolerance levels set for the critical operations they support
- Re-baseline the exit plan, including transition-out assistance and its cost

### Provider of TSA services

- Check whether any reverse TSAs make the buyer a material service provider to you
- List the subcontractors used to deliver TSA services, which become the recipient's fourth parties, and accept liability for their failure, which CPS 230 requires the agreement to place on the provider
- Price the reporting, audit support and continuity testing the recipient will require
- Identify any TSA service delivered from offshore, including by subcontractors or by personnel located overseas

### Both parties, at signing

- Draft TSA schedules to CPS 230 from the start, rather than amending after completion
- Agree who notifies APRA, and when, including prior notice for any offshore delivery, as part of the completion checklist
- Allow time before signing for due diligence on the provider and, where the TSA outsources a critical operation, internal audit review of the proposed arrangement

## Implications for deals in progress

For transactions now in negotiation, CPS 230 changes the economics of TSAs. Compliant TSAs cost more to provide and take longer to agree, which affects both the TSA fee and the timetable to completion. Where proposed TSA terms do not meet CPS 230, the cost of closing the gap flows into the TSA fee or the purchase price.

The practical response is to bring TSA design forward into diligence and treat it as a workstream with its own owner, rather than a schedule finalised the week before signing. Three steps sit before signing and add to the timetable: due diligence on the provider, internal audit review of any proposed arrangement that outsources a critical operation, and prior notice to APRA where any service is delivered offshore.

APRA finalised targeted amendments to CPS 230 on 30 April 2026, effective 1 July 2026. They relieve arrangements with certain categories of provider (government agencies, regulators, central banks, financial market exchanges, operators of clearing and settlement facilities, operators of payment systems and schemes, and financial messaging infrastructures) from specified contractual requirements, where the arrangement uses standardised terms or has no formal agreement. TSAs between commercial parties are not affected.
