## Wealth is back on the bank agenda

On 9 November ANZ reported full-year cash profit of A$7.6 billion, up 10%. The more consequential news was in the Australian Financial Review's Chanticleer column the same day, where chief executive Nuno Matos said that "sooner or later, wealth management has to be part of the banking industry again" and confirmed the bank is reviewing its options. The column described this as the growth bet that follows his cost programme.

ANZ has announced nothing, and one chief executive's view does not make an industry trend. The question is wider than one bank, though. Between 2018 and 2023 the four majors sold or wound down most of their wealth operations, and each kept something different: Westpac its BT Panorama platform, NAB its JBWere business, CBA a minority stake in Colonial First State. Any bank that decides to grow in wealth starts from whatever its own exit left behind.

Chanticleer asked two practical questions: how a bank avoids the cross-selling and conflict problems of the past, and how much strain re-entry puts on a leaner organisation. This article takes up both from the separation side. I led separations of this kind for the vendor at AMP, covering the advice business sold to Entireti and the AMP Capital businesses sold to Dexus and DigitalBridge. The work that ran longest in those programmes is the work a re-entry business case tends to leave out.

## What the business case usually misses

Re-entry cases are built on revenue, deposits and cross-sell. Whether they hold depends on six costs, most of which come from the separation history of the business being bought or partnered with.

1. **The vendor's TSAs.** A wealth business sold out of a larger group is often still running on its former parent's services. Those agreements have exit dates and step-up pricing, and the service descriptions are seldom complete. The buyer inherits the exit programme and its cost.
2. **Standalone cost.** A platform or super business carved out in recent years was sized for a parent it no longer has. Its cost base once the TSAs end can differ materially from the figure in the information memorandum.
3. **Trustee conflicts.** A bank that distributes a super product and also supplies services to its trustee creates a conflict the trustee has to manage. That constrains the deal structure and the service agreements as well as board composition.
4. **Remediation tails.** Indemnities given in the earlier sales may be capped, may have expired, or may sit with an entity that no longer has the balance sheet to honour them. Diligence has to trace each one to a counterparty that can pay.
5. **Data and consent.** The commercial logic depends on using bank customer data to serve wealth customers. Whether existing consents and privacy positions allow that is a legal question to settle before price.
6. **Programme capacity.** Re-entry draws on the same programme leaders, technology teams and risk functions as whatever the bank is already integrating. ANZ is the current example: the AFR reports it will spend next year completing the Suncorp Bank integration and consolidating three retail platforms into one, with its growth phase starting in FY28.

## What the exits separated

Each exit cut eight kinds of connection between a bank and its wealth operations. The table sets out what was separated and the decision each one now requires.

| Connection | What the exits separated | What re-entry has to decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Licences | AFSLs, RSE licences and trustee boards moved to the buyer | Whether the bank holds licences again or distributes under someone else's |
| Trustee governance | Related-party arrangements between bank and trustee were unwound | How the trustee manages the conflict if the bank is both distributor and service provider |
| Platforms and administration | Wrap and master trust platforms, registries and administration contracts moved, usually under long TSAs | Own, rent or partner for the platform; who carries the technology debt |
| Advice and distribution | Employed planners, referral flows from branches and relationship managers, and dealer group links were cut | Employed advisers, a referral partnership or digital advice only |
| Customer data and consent | Customer records were split, with consent and privacy positions set per business | What data can flow between bank and wealth provider, and on what consent basis |
| Remediation | Fee-for-no-service and other remediation stayed with the vendor, typically backed by indemnities | Whether an acquisition brings historical conduct exposure back |
| Shared services | Finance, risk, HR, technology infrastructure and procurement were stood up separately or provided under TSA | Which services the bank gives to or takes from the wealth business, and which are material arrangements under CPS 230 |
| Brand | Bank brands were removed from products, statements and marketing | Co-branding, white-labelling or full bank branding, and the conduct risk each carries |

In the exits, trustee governance and remediation took longest to resolve.

## Three routes back in

A bank can build, partner or buy. Each route rebuilds a different set of connections and puts the hard work at a different point.

| | Build | Partner | Buy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it looks like | Private bank and relationship-manager growth, in-house investment products, digital advice | Referral or white-label agreement with a platform, super fund, insurer or advice network | Acquire a platform, adviser network, super business or a whole wealth group |
| Connections rebuilt | Distribution, brand, some data | Distribution and data, under contract | Most or all eight |
| Where the work sits | Operating model and hiring, over years | Contract design, data flows and conduct controls, up front | Diligence, separation from the vendor, then integration |
| Regulatory weight | Lowest; within existing licences | Moderate; a CPS 230 material arrangement if the bank relies on the partner | Highest; licences, trustee obligations, regulatory approvals, historical conduct |
| Speed to scale | Slowest | Fast to launch, capped by the partner | Fastest to scale, slowest to stabilise |

ANZ has not chosen. Matos told the AFR the bank is still working through whether it would manufacture or distribute, and where it would sit between execution and advice. Most public commentary assumes an acquisition.

The partner route gets less attention than it warrants. It gives a bank distribution economics without taking back trustee and remediation exposure. The condition is that the agreement is drafted with the discipline of a TSA: defined services, service levels, data flows, step-in rights and exit rights. Over a ten-year term that is how it will operate.

On the buy route, few wealth businesses come to market as a clean unit. Most are still tied to a former parent or to sister businesses through shared platforms, trustees and service agreements. Separation work comes first and integration second.

Regulation bears on all three routes. The advice reforms announced in August 2026 leave banks outside the new adviser class and would put a compensation obligation behind platform trustees. We cover that in a companion article, [The advice reforms leave banks out: what that means for re-entry into wealth](/insights/advice-reforms-bank-re-entry/).

## Questions for the board

1. Which of the eight connections must we own for the economics to work, and which can we rent?
2. Are we prepared to hold trustee and licence obligations again, and what conduct exposure comes with them?
3. If we buy, what is the target still taking from its former parent, and what does it cost to exit those services?
4. If we partner, is the partner a material service provider under CPS 230, and do we have the exit rights that implies?
5. Can we resource this alongside our current integrations, or does it wait?

These questions are cheapest to answer in diligence. The work is specific: trace each indemnity to a counterparty that can pay, cost the exit from every TSA the target relies on, rebuild the standalone cost base from the service schedules up, and map which data flows the existing consents permit. Done before signing, it can still change the price or the structure.
